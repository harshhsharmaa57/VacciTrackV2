## 1. Official NIS 2025 Schedule (Birth to 16 Years)

### At Birth (Within 24 Hours)
- **BCG (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin):** 0.1 ml (0.05 ml if < 1 month) intradermal at left upper arm. Protects against severe childhood tuberculosis & TB meningitis.
- **OPV Zero Dose (Oral Polio Vaccine):** 2 drops orally.
- **Hepatitis B Birth Dose:** 0.5 ml intramuscular (anterolateral mid-thigh). Must be administered within 24 hours of birth to prevent perinatal transmission.

### At 6 Weeks (1.5 Months)
- **Pentavalent 1 (DTP + HepB + Hib):** 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh. Protects against Diphtheria, Pertussis (Whooping cough), Tetanus, Hepatitis B, and Haemophilus influenzae type B.
- **OPV 1:** 2 drops orally.
- **Rotavirus Vaccine 1 (RVV):** 5 drops (or 2.5 ml oral suspension depending on brand). Protects against severe rotaviral diarrhea.
- **fIPV 1 (Fractional Inactivated Polio Vaccine):** 0.1 ml intradermal, right upper arm.
- **PCV 1 (Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine):** 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh. Protects against pneumococcal pneumonia and meningitis.

### At 10 Weeks (2.5 Months)
- **Pentavalent 2:** 0.5 ml IM.
- **OPV 2:** 2 drops orally.
- **Rotavirus Vaccine 2:** 5 drops orally.

### At 14 Weeks (3.5 Months)
- **Pentavalent 3:** 0.5 ml IM.
- **OPV 3:** 2 drops orally.
- **Rotavirus Vaccine 3:** 5 drops orally.
- **fIPV 2:** 0.1 ml intradermal, right upper arm.
- **PCV 2:** 0.5 ml IM.

### At 9 to 12 Months
- **MR 1 (Measles & Rubella):** 0.5 ml subcutaneous, right upper arm.
- **PCV Booster:** 0.5 ml IM.
- **JE 1 (Japanese Encephalitis):** 0.5 ml SC, left upper arm (in endemic districts).
- **Vitamin A (1st Dose):** 1 ml (1 lakh IU) orally with spoon.

### At 16 to 24 Months
- **MR 2 (Measles & Rubella Dose 2):** 0.5 ml subcutaneous.
- **DPT Booster 1:** 0.5 ml IM, mid-thigh.
- **OPV Booster:** 2 drops orally.
- **JE 2:** 0.5 ml SC (in endemic districts).
- **Vitamin A (2nd to 9th Doses):** 2 ml (2 lakh IU) every 6 months up to 5 years of age.

### At 5 to 6 Years
- **DPT Booster 2:** 0.5 ml IM, left upper arm (deltoid).

### At 10 Years & 16 Years
- **Td (Tetanus & adult Diphtheria):** 0.5 ml IM, upper arm (replaces Tetanus Toxoid / TT).

## 2. Post-Vaccination Fever & AEFI (Adverse Events Following Immunization)

### Common Symptoms
- Mild fever (99°F - 101°F) lasting 24 to 48 hours is standard and reflects immune antibody building, especially after Pentavalent or DPT.
- Localized tenderness, redness, or a small hard nodule at the injection site.
- Mild irritability, crankiness, or increased sleepiness.

### Home Care Protocol
1. **Light Clothing:** Dress the infant in loose, breathable cotton clothing.
2. **Hydration:** Continue frequent on-demand breastfeeding. For babies older than 6 months, offer water and fluids.
3. **Cold Compress for Swelling:** Apply a clean, cold cloth or ice pack wrapped in a clean cotton cloth for 5-10 minutes. Do NOT massage or apply balm/ointments.
4. **Lukewarm Sponge for Fever:** Dampen a washcloth in lukewarm water and gently sponge the forehead and neck. Never use ice or alcohol.
5. **Medication:** Infant Paracetamol drops (PCM) may be given only according to weight as prescribed by the pediatrician. Never administer Aspirin or Ibuprofen without doctor instruction.

### Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Emergency Medical Care Immediately)
- Temperature exceeding 102°F (38.9°C) or fever persisting beyond 48 hours.
- Inconsolable, high-pitched persistent crying lasting more than 3 hours.
- Extreme lethargy, difficulty waking up, convulsions/seizures, or refusal to feed.
- Allergic reaction symptoms: facial swelling, hives, difficulty breathing.

## 3. Specific Vaccines Frequently Asked Questions

### BCG Scar Formation
- A BCG scar takes **6 to 12 weeks** to develop.
- Around week 2-3, a small red nodule appears. By week 5-6, it forms a small pustule which may weep watery fluid.
- **Rule:** Do NOT press, squeeze, rub, or apply turmeric/ointments. Keep it dry and clean.
- If no scar appears by 12 weeks: NIS 2025 guidelines state that around 10% of infants develop protection without a visible scar. Do NOT revaccinate without consulting your pediatrician.

### Bathing After Vaccination
- Yes, a baby can be given a warm sponge or gentle bath after vaccination.
- Avoid vigorously scrubbing the injection site or using hot water on swollen areas.

### OPV vs IPV (Oral Polio vs Injected Polio)
- **OPV (Oral Polio Vaccine - Sabin):** Live attenuated, given as 2 oral drops. Provides gut mucosal immunity (stops virus transmission in the community).
- **fIPV (Fractional Inactivated Polio Vaccine - Salk):** Killed virus given as a 0.1 ml injection in the skin. Provides strong humoral blood immunity and prevents paralysis. Both together provide complete immunity.

### Catch-Up Schedule for Missed Doses
- Under Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) NIS 2025, if a scheduled vaccine is delayed or missed:
- **Do NOT restart the entire course.** Continue from where the schedule was interrupted.
- Observe minimum inter-dose intervals: Pentavalent doses require a minimum 4-week gap between consecutive doses.
- Pentavalent can be administered up to 1 year of age. If older than 1 year, DPT is given.
